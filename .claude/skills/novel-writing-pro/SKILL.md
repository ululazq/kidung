# Novel Writing Pro — Blueprint-Based Storycraft

Reusable writing skill. Use when: outlining, expanding story ideas into chapter beats, developing protagonist/antagonist dynamics, building world rules and magic systems, writing scenes with cause-and-effect, maintaining consistency across plot and theme.

## Core Story Rules

1. **Define the central tension first.** A modern protagonist collides with an older force, technology, or secret.
2. **Build around a strong thematic anchor.** One core theme: identity, sacrifice, power, memory, freedom, belonging.
3. **Use layered antagonism.** External threat + internal threat (protagonist's own body/doubt) + political threat (hidden organization, manipulator).
4. **Make worldbuilding feel lived-in.** Place magic inside concrete locations; use cultural anchors; show human infrastructure interacting with the extraordinary.
5. **Quantify cost.** Define a clear, repeatable price for power: aging, memory loss, reputation, sanity, relationships. Cost must escalate visibly.
6. **Keep stakes personal.** Tie conflict to protagonist's friends, home, or identity. Make the cost visible to other characters.
7. **Structure in three acts plus milestone arcs.** Act I: awakening/disruption/first refusal. Act II: discovery/escalation/politics. Act III: confrontation/choice/resolution. Use midpoint reversal + escalating costs.
8. **The Vessel Principle.** The strongest twist: the protagonist is secretly central to the antagonist's plan (the protagonist IS the container/tool/key). Plant it in Act I with a small detail; reveal at midpoint.

## Power System Template

- **Rule system:** how the power works, activation condition.
- **Limitations:** what it cannot do.
- **Cost:** quantify per use (e.g., 1 day aging per minor skill, 1 year per karma severing; 1 memory fragment per name housed).
- **Scope:** geography and social boundaries.
- **Culture:** rituals, history, local details.
- **Technology:** how modern life interacts with the supernatural.

## Character Archetypes

- **Protagonist / Host:** begins uncertain, grows through cost.
- **Ancient/Outside Force:** powerful, alien, not fully trustworthy (may become ally).
- **Mentor / Ally:** reveals hidden rules; often sacrificed or lost at Act II climax.
- **Rival / Antagonist:** challenges method or ideology; may become fragile ally at Act III.
- **Manipulator / Puppet Master:** controls the larger system; reveal their origin as sympathetic (they are a victim of the same system).
- **Victim / Wildcard:** provides emotional stakes or a mirror.

## Scene Templates

1. **The Awakening Scene:** ordinary world → extraordinary intrudes gradually → jarring new awareness.
2. **The Cost Scene:** aftermath of using power; physical/emotional/social; concrete sensory detail; protagonist realizes price is real.
3. **The Political/Worldbuilding Scene:** faction/network intro; dialogue reveals rules and hidden agendas; ends with new objective.
4. **The Choice Scene:** clear dilemma; weigh outcomes; growth shown through choice; consequences inevitable.
5. **The Revelation Scene (midpoint):** one truth inverts the story (betrayal, protagonist's secret role, antagonist's origin).
6. **The Sacrifice Scene (Act III):** protagonist gives up something essential; the theme resolves through that choice.

## Quality Gates (per chapter)

- **Length:** 2,000–3,000 kata (chapter minimum 1,800; pivotal scenes up to 5,500).
- **Sensory anchors:** minimum 3 concrete sensory details (smell, sound, texture, temperature) per chapter.
- **Scene beats:** each chapter = 2-4 scenes with clear cause→effect.
- **Dual voice:** if dual consciousness, alternate internal monologue; if atmospheric POV, keep the register consistent.
- **End hook:** every chapter ends with a question, threat, or emotional beat that pulls to next chapter.
- **No info-dump:** rules revealed through action/dialogue, never paragraphs of exposition.

## Consistency Ledger (mandatory)

Before writing each chapter, maintain and check:

1. **Names:** characters, places, objects — exact spelling (e.g., Nyala, Stasiun 13, Midnight Parlor, Kolektor/Luruh).
2. **Rules:** power mechanics + costs — never contradict established numbers.
3. **Timeline:** days elapsed per chapter must add up; aging/cost tracked cumulatively.
4. **Factions/alliances:** who knows what, who trusts whom — no sudden unearned shifts.
5. **Foreshadowing:** Act I details must pay off by Act III.

## Plot-Hole Prevention Checklist

After every 3 chapters, verify:

- [ ] Do costs stay consistent with the established ledger?
- [ ] Does any character know something they shouldn't?
- [ ] Does any power solve a problem it couldn't before (no new rule invented mid-story)?
- [ ] Are all Act I promises (objects, names, symbols) either paid off or intentionally ambiguous?
- [ ] Does the antagonist's plan still make sense given what the protagonists did?
- [ ] Timeline consistent (no impossible travel/time)?
- [ ] Does each character's motivation still drive their actions?

## Revision Protocol

1. Write chapters in order.
2. After every 3 chapters: run plot-hole checklist; fix forward, never retcon silently — if a fix changes earlier chapters, edit those files too.
3. Before final push: read Chapter 1 and final chapter together; the ending must echo the beginning.

## Writing Guidelines

- Active voice, specific detail.
- Dialogue character-distinct (each character has a verbal signature).
- Anchor weirdness in ordinary touchpoints (kopi, hujan, neon, macet).
- Short scenes build momentum in Act II; longer introspective passages for emotional beats.
- Chapter-ending italic/poetic coda (optional, per-novel register) for thematic echo.

## Per-Novel Style Registers (proven)

- **The Host:** dark, gritty, philosophical; dual POV (Arif/Rahwana); skills in English (Mind Palace, Karma Sense, Shadow Step, Soul Whisper); cost = biological aging; antagonist = fusion of consciousness (Agung/Brahma shard); resolution = shared sacrifice.
- **Lantern of Night:** moody, atmospheric, literary; sensory-heavy; single POV (Mila); supernatural terms in Indonesian (Nyala, bayangan, nama); cost = memory fragments per name housed; antagonist = The Collector (Luruh) who harvests names; twist = protagonist is a living vessel (half-name). Resolution = giving the antagonist a home instead of destroying her.

## How to Adapt

Replace setting, power mechanism, cost, factions, endgame. Keep: disturbance → exploration → confrontation; character map; theme guide; quality gates; consistency ledger.

## Notes on Reuse

This skill is a guide, not a formula. The best novels follow the core rules, then surprise the reader with specific details.
